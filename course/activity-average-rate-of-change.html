    Worksheet Activity: Average Rate of Change
    

    1.

    Find the average rate of change of \(\displaystyle g(x) = \frac{36}{x}\) on the interval \([2,6]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First, evaluate the function at the endpoints \(x = 6\) and \(x = 2\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} g(6) \amp= \frac{36}{6} = 6\\ g(2) \amp= \frac{36}{2} = 18 \end{align*}
    Next, apply the average rate of change formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{g(6)-g(2)}{6-2} \amp= \frac{6-18}{4}\\ \amp= \frac{-12}{4}\\ \amp= -3 \end{align*}
    The average rate of change is \(-3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Find the average rate of change of \(k(x) = 2x^2 - 5\) on the interval \([-1,3]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Evaluate the function at the endpoints \(x = 3\) and \(x = -1\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} k(3) \amp= 2(3)^2 - 5 = 2(9) - 5 = 18 - 5 = 13\\ k(-1) \amp= 2(-1)^2 - 5 = 2(1) - 5 = 2 - 5 = -3 \end{align*}
    Apply the formula, being very careful with subtracting negative numbers in the denominator:
    \begin{align*} \frac{k(3)-k(-1)}{3-(-1)} \amp= \frac{13 - (-3)}{3 - (-1)}\\ \amp= \frac{13 + 3}{3 + 1}\\ \amp= \frac{16}{4}\\ \amp= 4 \end{align*}
    The average rate of change is \(4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Find the average rate of change of \(m(x) = 12\sqrt{x}\) on the interval \([1,9]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Evaluate the function at the endpoints \(x = 9\) and \(x = 1\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} m(9) \amp= 12\sqrt{9} = 12(3) = 36\\ m(1) \amp= 12\sqrt{1} = 12(1) = 12 \end{align*}
    Apply the average rate of change formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{m(9)-m(1)}{9-1} \amp= \frac{36-12}{8}\\ \amp= \frac{24}{8}\\ \amp= 3 \end{align*}
    The average rate of change is \(3\text{.}\)

    4.

    Worked Example: A new app is gaining subscribers. The total number of subscribers \(N(x)\) after \(x\) weeks is given below. Find the average rate of change from \(x=3\) to \(x=12\) and interpret it.
    🔗
    \(x\) (weeks) \(0\) \(3\) \(6\) \(9\) \(12\)
    \(N(x)\) (subscribers) \(120\) \(210\) \(340\) \(480\) \(660\)
    Solution: Read the two endpoint columns, \(N(3) = 210\) and \(N(12) = 660\text{.}\) Then:
    \begin{align*} \frac{N(12)-N(3)}{12-3} \amp= \frac{660-210}{9} = \frac{450}{9} = 50 \end{align*}
    🔗
    The average rate of change is \(50\) subscribers per week. Between week \(3\) and week \(12\text{,}\) the app gained an average of \(50\) new subscribers each week. The columns at \(x=6\) and \(x=9\) never entered the computation.
    🔗
    Practice: A large tank is being drained, and \(W(x)\) is the amount of water left in the tank after \(x\) minutes.
    🔗
    \(x\) (minutes) \(0\) \(5\) \(10\) \(15\) \(20\)
    \(W(x)\) (gallons) \(800\) \(620\) \(430\) \(290\) \(155\)

    (a)

    Determine the average rate of change of the water amount on this interval, and state its units.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using the table, \(W(20) = 155\) and \(W(5) = 620\text{.}\) Apply the formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{W(20)-W(5)}{20-5} \amp= \frac{155-620}{15}\\ \amp= \frac{-465}{15}\\ \amp= -31 \end{align*}
    The average rate of change is \(-31\) gallons per minute.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Describe the meaning of your result in the context of the question. Write a full sentence that does not use the letters \(W\) or \(x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Between \(5\) and \(20\) minutes after draining began, the tank lost an average of \(31\) gallons of water each minute.

    5.

    (a)

    Determine the average rate of change of the water amount on this interval.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using the table, \(W(10) = 430\) and \(W(0) = 800\text{.}\) Apply the formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{W(10)-W(0)}{10-0} \amp= \frac{430-800}{10}\\ \amp= \frac{-370}{10}\\ \amp= -37 \end{align*}
    The average rate of change is \(-37\) gallons per minute.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Compare your answer to the one from the previous interval. During which stretch of time was the tank draining faster? Explain how you can tell from the two numbers.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The tank drained faster over the first ten minutes. It was losing \(37\) gallons per minute there compared with \(31\) gallons per minute on the later interval. Both values are negative (indicating a loss of water), so the comparison is about which rate is farther from zero, not which is larger as a signed number. This demonstrates that the average rate of change depends heavily on the specific interval you choose.

    6.

    Worked Example: The graph of \(h(x)\) is shown below. Find the average rate of change of \(h\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    
    A downward opening parabola with a dashed line connecting the points (0,2) and (2,0).
    🔗
    Solution: Read the outputs off the graph: \(h(0) = 2\) and \(h(2) = 0\text{.}\) Then:
    \begin{align*} \frac{h(2)-h(0)}{2-0} \amp= \frac{0-2}{2} = -1 \end{align*}
    🔗
    The average rate of change is \(-1\text{.}\) Notice that this is exactly the slope of the dashed line drawn through the two points on the curve, which is what the average rate of change visually measures.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the average rate of change of \(f\) from \(x=-2\) to \(x=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Reading the points from the graph, we have \(f(0) = -2\) and \(f(-2) = 0\text{.}\) Applying the formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{f(0)-f(-2)}{0-(-2)} \amp= \frac{-2-0}{2}\\ \amp= \frac{-2}{2}\\ \amp= -1 \end{align*}
    The average rate of change is \(-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the average rate of change of \(f\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Reading the points from the graph, we have \(f(2) = 0\) and \(f(0) = -2\text{.}\) Applying the formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{f(2)-f(0)}{2-0} \amp= \frac{0-(-2)}{2}\\ \amp= \frac{2}{2}\\ \amp= 1 \end{align*}
    The average rate of change is \(1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Your two answers have opposite signs even though the graph is one smooth curve. Explain what each sign is telling you about the outputs on that interval.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The negative answer on the first interval tells us that the outputs decreased on the way from \(x=-2\) to \(x=0\text{.}\) The positive answer on the second interval tells us that the outputs increased from \(x=0\) to \(x=2\text{.}\) Because the curve turns around at the bottom vertex, the sign of the average rate of change flips depending on which side of the low point the interval is located.

    7.

    Worked Example: Suppose \(f(t) = 150 - 16t^2\) is the height in feet of an object dropped from a building after \(t\) seconds. Find the average rate of change of \(f\) on \([0,3]\) and describe what it means.
    🔗
    Solution: Evaluate at both endpoints.
    \begin{align*} f(0) \amp= 150 - 16(0)^2 = 150\\ f(3) \amp= 150 - 16(3)^2 = 150 - 144 = 6 \end{align*}
    Then apply the formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{f(3)-f(0)}{3-0} \amp= \frac{6-150}{3} = \frac{-144}{3} = -48 \end{align*}
    🔗
    The average rate of change is \(-48\) feet per second. During the first \(3\) seconds of the fall, the object drops an average of \(48\) feet each second, so its average velocity over that stretch is \(48\) feet per second downward.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the average rate of change of \(s\) on \([1,4]\text{,}\) and state its units.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Evaluate the function at \(t = 4\) and \(t = 1\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} s(4) \amp= 300 - 16(4)^2 = 300 - 16(16) = 300 - 256 = 44\\ s(1) \amp= 300 - 16(1)^2 = 300 - 16 = 284 \end{align*}
    Apply the formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{s(4)-s(1)}{4-1} \amp= \frac{44-284}{3}\\ \amp= \frac{-240}{3}\\ \amp= -80 \end{align*}
    The average rate of change is \(-80\) feet per second.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Describe in words what your answer to part (a) means. Your sentence should mention the object, the time, and the direction it is moving.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Between \(1\) and \(4\) seconds after the object was dropped, its height decreased by an average of \(80\) feet each second, meaning it was moving downward at an average velocity of \(80\) ft/s.

    8.

    (a)

    Find the average rate of change of \(V\) on \([0,3]\text{,}\) and state its units.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Evaluate the function at \(t = 3\) and \(t = 0\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} V(3) \amp= 32000 - 2400(3) = 32000 - 7200 = 24800\\ V(0) \amp= 32000 - 2400(0) = 32000 \end{align*}
    Apply the formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{V(3)-V(0)}{3-0} \amp= \frac{24800-32000}{3}\\ \amp= \frac{-7200}{3}\\ \amp= -2400 \end{align*}
    The average rate of change is \(-2400\) dollars per year.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the average rate of change of \(V\) on \([5,9]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Evaluate the function at \(t = 9\) and \(t = 5\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} V(9) \amp= 32000 - 2400(9) = 32000 - 21600 = 10400\\ V(5) \amp= 32000 - 2400(5) = 32000 - 12000 = 20000 \end{align*}
    Apply the formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{V(9)-V(5)}{9-5} \amp= \frac{10400-20000}{4}\\ \amp= \frac{-9600}{4}\\ \amp= -2400 \end{align*}
    The average rate of change is \(-2400\) dollars per year.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    You should have gotten the same answer twice. What is it about this particular function that makes the average rate of change come out the same on every interval?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(V\) is a linear function, and its slope is \(-2400\text{.}\) For any line, the average rate of change between any two points is exactly equal to the constant slope of the line, which is why the specific interval chosen does not matter. For every other non-linear function type in this activity, the interval will change the result.
