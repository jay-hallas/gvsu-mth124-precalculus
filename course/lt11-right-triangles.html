    Worksheet Learning Target 11: Right Triangles
    

    Objectives

    • Given a right triangle with partial information about its sides and angles, I can determine the missing information for the remaining sides and angles, even if the quantities involve an unknown variable.
      🔗
    🔗

    1. Problem 1: Two Sides Known.

    In the right triangle below, the right angle is at \(C\text{.}\) All angles are measured in degrees.
    🔗
    
    Right triangle with vertex C at the lower left, vertex B at the lower right, and vertex A directly above C. The vertical leg from C to A has length 8. The horizontal leg from C to B is labeled a. The hypotenuse from A to B has length 17. A small square at C marks the right angle.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the value of \(a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    By the Pythagorean Theorem,
    \begin{align*} a^2 + 8^2 \amp= 17^2\\ a^2 \amp= 289 - 64 = 225\\ a \amp= 15 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the exact values of \(\csc(A)\text{,}\) \(\sec(A)\text{,}\) \(\tan(A)\text{,}\) \(\sin(B)\text{,}\) \(\cos(B)\text{,}\) and \(\cot(B)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    From angle \(A\text{,}\) the opposite side is \(15\text{,}\) the adjacent side is \(8\text{,}\) and the hypotenuse is \(17\text{.}\) From angle \(B\text{,}\) the opposite side is \(8\) and the adjacent side is \(15\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \(\csc(A) = \frac{17}{15}\text{,}\) \(\sec(A) = \frac{17}{8}\text{,}\) \(\tan(A) = \frac{15}{8}\text{,}\) \(\sin(B) = \frac{8}{17}\text{,}\) \(\cos(B) = \frac{15}{17}\text{,}\) and \(\cot(B) = \frac{15}{8}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find the angles \(A\) and \(B\) in degrees. Round to two decimal places.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(A = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{15}{8}\right) \approx 61.93^\circ\text{.}\) Since the angles of a triangle add to \(180^\circ\) and \(C = 90^\circ\text{,}\) \(B = 90^\circ - A \approx 28.07^\circ\text{.}\) As a check, \(\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{8}{17}\right) \approx 28.07^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Problem 2: One Angle and One Side Known.

    In the right triangle below, the right angle is at \(C\text{,}\) angle \(A\) measures \(35^\circ\text{,}\) and side \(AC\) has length \(12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    
    Right triangle with vertex C at the lower left, vertex A at the lower right, and vertex B directly above C. The horizontal leg from C to A has length 12. The vertical leg from C to B is labeled y. The hypotenuse from A to B is labeled z. The angle at A is 35 degrees, and a small square at C marks the right angle.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the angle \(B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(B = 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find \(y\text{.}\) Give the exact value using a trig function, then round to two decimal places.
    🔗
    Answer.
    From angle \(A\text{,}\) \(y\) is opposite and \(12\) is adjacent, so \(\tan(35^\circ) = \frac{y}{12}\text{.}\) Then \(y = 12\tan(35^\circ) \approx 8.40\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find \(z\text{.}\) Give the exact value using a trig function, then round to two decimal places.
    🔗
    Answer.
    From angle \(A\text{,}\) \(12\) is adjacent and \(z\) is the hypotenuse, so \(\cos(35^\circ) = \frac{12}{z}\text{.}\) Then \(z = \frac{12}{\cos(35^\circ)} \approx 14.65\text{.}\) As a check, \(8.40^2 + 12^2 \approx 214.6\) and \(14.65^2 \approx 214.6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. Problem 3: Sides in Terms of a Variable.

    The legs of a right triangle have lengths \(x\) and \(x + 7\text{,}\) and the hypotenuse has length \(13\text{.}\)
    🔗
    
    Right triangle with the right angle at the lower left. The vertical leg is labeled x, the horizontal leg is labeled x plus 7, and the hypotenuse is labeled 13. The angle at the lower right vertex is labeled theta.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find \(x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    By the Pythagorean Theorem,
    \begin{align*} x^2 + (x + 7)^2 \amp= 13^2\\ 2x^2 + 14x + 49 \amp= 169\\ x^2 + 7x - 60 \amp= 0\\ (x + 12)(x - 5) \amp= 0 \end{align*}
    🔗
    So \(x = 5\) or \(x = -12\text{.}\) A side length cannot be negative, so \(x = 5\text{,}\) and the legs are \(5\) and \(12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find \(\sin(\theta)\text{,}\) \(\cos(\theta)\text{,}\) and \(\theta\) in degrees.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The side opposite \(\theta\) is \(5\) and the adjacent side is \(12\text{,}\) so \(\sin(\theta) = \frac{5}{13}\) and \(\cos(\theta) = \frac{12}{13}\text{.}\) Then \(\theta = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{13}\right) \approx 22.62^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Problem 4: An Unknown Hypotenuse.

    A right triangle has a hypotenuse of length \(x\) and an angle of \(40^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    
    Right triangle with the right angle at the lower left. The angle at the lower right vertex is 40 degrees. The hypotenuse is labeled x. The legs are not labeled.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write the lengths of both legs in terms of \(x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The leg opposite the \(40^\circ\) angle is \(x\sin(40^\circ)\text{,}\) since \(\sin(40^\circ) = \frac{\text{opposite}}{x}\text{.}\) The leg adjacent to it is \(x\cos(40^\circ)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    The perimeter of the triangle is \(30\text{.}\) Find \(x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Add the three sides and factor out \(x\text{.}\)
    \begin{align*} x + x\sin(40^\circ) + x\cos(40^\circ) \amp= 30\\ x\left(1 + \sin(40^\circ) + \cos(40^\circ)\right) \amp= 30\\ x \amp= \frac{30}{1 + \sin(40^\circ) + \cos(40^\circ)} \approx 12.45 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. Problem 5: A Ladder.

    Problems 5, 7, and 8 do not include a picture. Draw one before you start.
    🔗
    Ladder safety guidelines recommend setting up an extension ladder at an angle of about \(75^\circ\) with the ground. A \(24\)-foot ladder leans against a house at this angle.
    🔗

    (a)

    How high up the house does the ladder reach?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The ladder is the hypotenuse and the height is opposite the \(75^\circ\) angle, so \(\sin(75^\circ) = \frac{h}{24}\text{.}\) Then \(h = 24\sin(75^\circ) \approx 23.18\) feet.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    How far from the house should the base of the ladder be placed?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The distance is adjacent to the \(75^\circ\) angle, so it is \(24\cos(75^\circ) \approx 6.21\) feet. This matches the rule of thumb of about \(1\) foot out for every \(4\) feet up.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Problem 6: Two Angles of Elevation.

    From point \(P\text{,}\) the angle of elevation to the top of a building is \(40^\circ\text{.}\) After walking \(50\) feet straight toward the building to point \(Q\text{,}\) the angle of elevation is \(55^\circ\text{.}\) Let \(h\) be the height of the building and \(d\) the distance from \(Q\) to the building.
    🔗
    
    A vertical building of height h stands at the right. Point P is on the ground at the left, and point Q is 50 feet closer to the building. The distance from Q to the building is d. A line from P to the top of the building makes a 40 degree angle with the ground, and a line from Q to the top makes a 55 degree angle.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write two equations involving \(h\) and \(d\text{,}\) one for each right triangle.
    🔗
    Answer.
    From \(Q\text{,}\) \(\tan(55^\circ) = \frac{h}{d}\text{,}\) so \(h = d\tan(55^\circ)\text{.}\) From \(P\text{,}\) the horizontal distance is \(d + 50\text{,}\) so \(h = (d + 50)\tan(40^\circ)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Solve for \(d\text{,}\) then find the height of the building.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Set the two expressions for \(h\) equal.
    \begin{align*} d\tan(55^\circ) \amp= d\tan(40^\circ) + 50\tan(40^\circ)\\ d\left(\tan(55^\circ) - \tan(40^\circ)\right) \amp= 50\tan(40^\circ)\\ d \amp= \frac{50\tan(40^\circ)}{\tan(55^\circ) - \tan(40^\circ)} \approx 71.23 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Then \(h = d\tan(55^\circ) \approx 101.72\) feet.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    7. Problem 7: Watching a Launch.

    A camera is set up on level ground \(3\) miles from a rocket’s launch pad. The rocket rises straight up.
    🔗

    (a)

    What is the angle of elevation from the camera to the rocket when the rocket is \(4\) miles high?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The height \(4\) is opposite the angle and the distance \(3\) is adjacent, so \(\tan(\theta) = \frac{4}{3}\) and \(\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{4}{3}\right) \approx 53.13^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    How far is the rocket from the camera at that moment?
    🔗
    Answer.
    By the Pythagorean Theorem, the distance is \(\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5\) miles.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    8. Problem 8: A Wheelchair Ramp.

    The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) requires that a wheelchair ramp rise no more than \(1\) inch for every \(12\) inches of horizontal distance. A ramp is being built to the front door of a building, which is \(30\) inches above the sidewalk.
    🔗

    (a)

    What is the steepest angle a ramp can make with the ground and still meet this rule?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The rise is opposite the angle and the horizontal distance is adjacent, so \(\tan(\theta) = \frac{1}{12}\) and \(\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{12}\right) \approx 4.76^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    At this steepest angle, how much horizontal distance does the ramp need? How long is the ramp itself?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The horizontal distance is \(12 \cdot 30 = 360\) inches, or \(30\) feet. The ramp is the hypotenuse, so its length is \(\sqrt{360^2 + 30^2} \approx 361.25\) inches, a little more than \(30\) feet. Using trig instead, the length is \(\frac{30}{\sin(4.76^\circ)} \approx 361.2\) inches.
    🔗
