    Worksheet Activity: Linear Functions

    Equations of Lines.

    Linear Equations.

    A linear function can be written as \(y = mx + b\text{,}\) where \(m\) is the slope (constant rate of change) and \(b\) is the \(y\)-intercept (starting value). Alternatively, if you know the slope \(m\) and any point \((x_1, y_1)\) on the line, you can use the point-slope form: \(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    1.

    Find an equation of a line passing through the points \((-2,-1)\) and \((1,17)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(y = 6x + 11\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    First, find the slope. Then use point-slope form with \((-2,-1)\text{.}\)
    \begin{align*} m \amp= \frac{17-(-1)}{1-(-2)} = \frac{18}{3} = 6\\ y - (-1) \amp= 6(x - (-2))\\ y + 1 \amp= 6(x + 2)\\ y + 1 \amp= 6x + 12\\ y \amp= 6x + 11 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Find an equation of a line with slope \(\displaystyle \frac{1}{3}\) and \(y\)-intercept \(-5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(y = \frac{1}{3}x - 5\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    We are given \(m = \frac{1}{3}\) and \(b = -5\) directly. Substituting these into \(y = mx + b\) gives \(y = \frac{1}{3}x - 5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Linear Tables.

    Example 9. Worked Example.

    To determine if a table of values represents a linear function, check if the average rate of change between every pair of points is constant. Be careful: the \(x\)-values might not increase by a uniform amount, so you must always compute \(\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Which of the following tables could represent a linear function? Show your rate calculations to justify your answer.
    🔗
    \(x\) \(3\) \(4\) \(5\) \(7\)
    \(f(x)\) \(-2\) \(-5\) \(-8\) \(-11\)
    \(x\) \(1\) \(2\) \(5\) \(7\)
    \(g(x)\) \(0.25\) \(0.5\) \(1.25\) \(1.75\)
    Solution.
    Table for \(f(x)\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} \frac{-5-(-2)}{4-3} \amp= \frac{-3}{1} = -3\\ \frac{-8-(-5)}{5-4} \amp= \frac{-3}{1} = -3\\ \frac{-11-(-8)}{7-5} \amp= \frac{-3}{2} = -1.5 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Because the rate of change drops from \(-3\) to \(-1.5\text{,}\) \(f(x)\) is not linear. Students often incorrectly assume it is linear by only looking at the pattern in the \(y\)-values (which decrease by \(3\) each time), failing to notice the jump from \(x=5\) to \(x=7\) in the input.
    🔗
    Table for \(g(x)\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} \frac{0.5-0.25}{2-1} \amp= \frac{0.25}{1} = 0.25\\ \frac{1.25-0.5}{5-2} \amp= \frac{0.75}{3} = 0.25\\ \frac{1.75-1.25}{7-5} \amp= \frac{0.5}{2} = 0.25 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Because the rate of change is consistently \(0.25\) between every pair of points, \(g(x)\) could be linear.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Make two \((x,y)\) tables of your own (with at least \(4\) points each). One table should represent a linear pattern, and the other should not.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Answers will vary. Ensure the linear table has a constant \(\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\) ratio, and the non-linear table does not.
    🔗
    🔗

    Models from Rate and Initial Value.

    Sign of the Slope.

    When reading a word problem, pay attention to whether the context describes growth or loss. If a quantity is increasing, the slope is positive. If a quantity is decreasing, the slope is negative.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Your phone plan comes with \(15\) GB of data, and you use about \(2.5\) GB per week.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write a linear equation for your remaining data \(D\) (in GB) after \(w\) weeks.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(D(w) = -2.5w + 15\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The starting data is \(15\) GB, so \(b = 15\text{.}\) The data is decreasing, so the rate of change is \(m = -2.5\text{.}\) The equation is \(D(w) = -2.5w + 15\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    After how many weeks will your data run out?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(6\) weeks
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The data runs out when \(D(w) = 0\text{.}\)
    \begin{align*} 0 \amp= -2.5w + 15\\ 2.5w \amp= 15\\ w \amp= 6 \end{align*}
    🔗
    The data runs out after \(6\) weeks.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    Suppose \(5\) years after buying a new car it is worth $21,250, and when it is \(20\) years old it is worth $10,000. Assume the price of the car depreciates linearly.
    🔗

    (a)

    Determine a linear function \(P(t)\) that gives the price \(t\) years after the car was purchased.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(P(t) = -750t + 25000\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Find the slope using \((5, 21250)\) and \((20, 10000)\text{,}\) then use point-slope form.
    \begin{align*} m \amp= \frac{10000-21250}{20-5} = \frac{-11250}{15} = -750\\ P - 21250 \amp= -750(t - 5)\\ P \amp= -750t + 3750 + 21250\\ P(t) \amp= -750t + 25000 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    What was the initial value of the car when it was purchased?
    🔗
    Answer.
    $25,000
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The initial value corresponds to \(t = 0\text{,}\) which is the vertical intercept. The car was originally purchased for $25,000.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    When will the car have no value? Based on this, what is a practical domain for your function?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(33.33\) years, \([0, 33.33]\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The car has no value when \(P(t) = 0\text{.}\)
    \begin{align*} 0 \amp= -750t + 25000\\ 750t \amp= 25000\\ t \amp= \frac{25000}{750} \approx 33.33 \end{align*}
    🔗
    The car has no value after about \(33.33\) years. The practical domain restricts the model to times before the car’s value becomes negative: \([0, 33.33]\text{.}\)
    🔗
