    Worksheet Learning Target 10: Solving Exponential and Logarithmic Equations
    

    Objectives

    • I can find exact solutions to equations involving exponential and logarithmic expressions in contextual settings.
      🔗
    🔗

    1. Problem 1: Base \(e\) and Base \(10\).

    Solve each equation for \(x\text{.}\) Give the exact solution first, then round to three decimal places.
    🔗

    (a)

    \(e^{3x} = 40\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Take \(\ln\) of both sides.
    \begin{align*} \ln\left(e^{3x}\right) \amp= \ln(40)\\ 3x \amp= \ln(40)\\ x \amp= \frac{\ln(40)}{3} \approx 1.230 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    \(5e^{x + 2} - 7 = 18\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Isolate the exponential expression first.
    \begin{align*} 5e^{x + 2} \amp= 25\\ e^{x + 2} \amp= 5\\ x + 2 \amp= \ln(5)\\ x \amp= \ln(5) - 2 \approx -0.391 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    \(10^{2x} = 500\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Take \(\log\) of both sides.
    \begin{align*} 2x \amp= \log(500)\\ x \amp= \frac{\log(500)}{2} \approx 1.349 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    \(4 \cdot 10^{x - 1} + 3 = 35\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} 4 \cdot 10^{x - 1} \amp= 32\\ 10^{x - 1} \amp= 8\\ x - 1 \amp= \log(8)\\ x \amp= 1 + \log(8) \approx 1.903 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Problem 2: Other Bases.

    Solve each equation for \(x\text{.}\) You can take \(\ln\) of both sides and use the power rule \(\ln\left(b^x\right) = x\ln(b)\text{,}\) or rewrite both sides with a common base when possible. Give exact solutions.
    🔗

    (a)

    \(2^x = 50\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} x\ln(2) \amp= \ln(50)\\ x \amp= \frac{\ln(50)}{\ln(2)} \approx 5.644 \end{align*}
    🔗
    This can also be written as \(x = \log_2(50)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    \(6 \cdot 3^{x - 1} = 90\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} 3^{x - 1} \amp= 15\\ (x - 1)\ln(3) \amp= \ln(15)\\ x \amp= 1 + \frac{\ln(15)}{\ln(3)} \approx 3.465 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    \(8^x = 4^{x + 1}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Both \(8\) and \(4\) are powers of \(2\text{.}\)
    \begin{align*} \left(2^3\right)^x \amp= \left(2^2\right)^{x + 1}\\ 2^{3x} \amp= 2^{2x + 2}\\ 3x \amp= 2x + 2\\ x \amp= 2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    \(5^{2x} = 3^{x + 1}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    There is no common base, so take \(\ln\) of both sides and collect the \(x\) terms.
    \begin{align*} 2x\ln(5) \amp= (x + 1)\ln(3)\\ 2x\ln(5) - x\ln(3) \amp= \ln(3)\\ x\left(2\ln(5) - \ln(3)\right) \amp= \ln(3)\\ x \amp= \frac{\ln(3)}{2\ln(5) - \ln(3)} \approx 0.518 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. Problem 3: Equations with Logarithms.

    Solve each equation for \(x\text{.}\) Isolate the logarithm, then rewrite the equation in exponential form. Give exact solutions.
    🔗

    (a)

    \(\ln(x) = 4\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    In exponential form, \(x = e^4 \approx 54.598\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    \(\log(3x - 2) = 2\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} 3x - 2 \amp= 10^2 = 100\\ x \amp= 34 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    \(2\ln(x + 1) - 3 = 5\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} 2\ln(x + 1) \amp= 8\\ \ln(x + 1) \amp= 4\\ x + 1 \amp= e^4\\ x \amp= e^4 - 1 \approx 53.598 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    \(\log_2(x) + 3 = 8\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\log_2(x) = 5\text{,}\) so \(x = 2^5 = 32\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Problem 4: Saving for a Goal.

    You deposit \(\$2{,}000\) into a savings account that earns \(4.5\%\) annual interest, compounded monthly. The balance after \(t\) years is \(A(t) = 2000\left(1 + \frac{0.045}{12}\right)^{12t}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    How long will it take for the balance to reach \(\$3{,}000\text{?}\) Give the exact answer, then round to two decimal places.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} 3000 \amp= 2000\left(1 + \tfrac{0.045}{12}\right)^{12t}\\ 1.5 \amp= (1.00375)^{12t}\\ \ln(1.5) \amp= 12t\ln(1.00375)\\ t \amp= \frac{\ln(1.5)}{12\ln(1.00375)} \approx 9.03 \end{align*}
    🔗
    It takes about \(9.03\) years.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    About how many monthly compounding periods is that? How can you get this number without solving a new equation?
    🔗
    Answer.
    Each year has \(12\) compounding periods, so \(12t = \frac{\ln(1.5)}{\ln(1.00375)} \approx 108.3\text{.}\) The balance passes \(\$3{,}000\) in the \(109\)th month.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. Problem 5: A Used Car.

    You buy a used car for \(\$18{,}000\text{.}\) Its value decreases by \(15\%\) each year.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write a function \(V(t)\) for the value of the car in dollars \(t\) years after you buy it.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(V(t) = 18000(0.85)^t\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    How long until the car is worth half of what you paid? Give the exact answer and a decimal approximation.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} 9000 \amp= 18000(0.85)^t\\ 0.5 \amp= (0.85)^t\\ t \amp= \frac{\ln(0.5)}{\ln(0.85)} \approx 4.27 \end{align*}
    🔗
    The car is worth half its price after about \(4.27\) years. Notice the answer does not depend on the price, only on the \(15\%\) rate.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Problem 6: Bacteria on a Countertop.

    Under ideal conditions, E. coli bacteria can double about every \(20\) minutes. A spill on a kitchen counter starts with \(100\) bacteria. The number of bacteria after \(t\) minutes is \(N(t) = 100 \cdot 2^{t/20}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    How long until there are \(1{,}000{,}000\) bacteria? Give the exact answer, then convert to hours.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} 1{,}000{,}000 \amp= 100 \cdot 2^{t/20}\\ 10{,}000 \amp= 2^{t/20}\\ \frac{t}{20}\ln(2) \amp= \ln(10{,}000)\\ t \amp= \frac{20\ln(10{,}000)}{\ln(2)} \approx 265.8 \end{align*}
    🔗
    That is about \(265.8\) minutes, or about \(4.4\) hours. Wipe up your spills.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Explain why the exponent in the model is \(\frac{t}{20}\) and not \(t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The population doubles once every \(20\) minutes, not every minute. In \(t\) minutes there are \(\frac{t}{20}\) doubling periods, so the factor of \(2\) is applied \(\frac{t}{20}\) times.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    7. Problem 7: Half-Life of a Medication.

    Ibuprofen has a half-life of about \(2\) hours in the body. After a \(400\) milligram dose, the amount remaining after \(t\) hours is \(A(t) = 400\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/2}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    When will \(50\) milligrams remain? Solve without a calculator.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} 50 \amp= 400\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/2}\\ \tfrac{1}{8} \amp= \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/2} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Since \(\frac{1}{8} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3\text{,}\) we need \(\frac{t}{2} = 3\text{,}\) so \(t = 6\) hours.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    When will \(30\) milligrams remain? Give the exact answer and a decimal approximation.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} 30 \amp= 400\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/2}\\ 0.075 \amp= (0.5)^{t/2}\\ \frac{t}{2}\ln(0.5) \amp= \ln(0.075)\\ t \amp= \frac{2\ln(0.075)}{\ln(0.5)} \approx 7.47 \end{align*}
    🔗
    About \(7.47\) hours after the dose.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    8. Problem 8: Waiting for Coffee to Cool.

    The temperature of a cup of coffee \(t\) minutes after it is poured is \(F(t) = 70 + 120e^{-0.04t}\) degrees Fahrenheit.
    🔗

    (a)

    What are the starting temperature of the coffee and the temperature of the room?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The coffee starts at \(F(0) = 70 + 120 = 190^\circ\text{F}\text{.}\) The room is \(70^\circ\text{F}\text{,}\) the value the temperature approaches.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    When will the coffee reach \(140^\circ\text{F}\text{?}\) Give the exact answer, then round to the nearest tenth of a minute.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} 140 \amp= 70 + 120e^{-0.04t}\\ \frac{70}{120} \amp= e^{-0.04t}\\ \ln\left(\frac{7}{12}\right) \amp= -0.04t\\ t \amp= \frac{\ln(7/12)}{-0.04} = 25\ln\left(\frac{12}{7}\right) \approx 13.5 \end{align*}
    🔗
    About \(13.5\) minutes.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Try to solve \(F(t) = 65\text{.}\) What goes wrong, and what does that mean for the coffee?
    🔗
    Answer.
    We get \(120e^{-0.04t} = -5\text{,}\) but \(e^{-0.04t}\) is always positive, so there is no solution. The coffee can never cool below the room temperature of \(70^\circ\text{F}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    9. Problem 9: Acidity and pH.

    The pH of a liquid is defined by \(\text{pH} = -\log[H^+]\text{,}\) where \([H^+]\) is the concentration of hydrogen ions in moles per liter. Black coffee has a pH of about \(5.0\text{,}\) and lemon juice has a pH of about \(2.3\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the hydrogen ion concentration of black coffee and of lemon juice. Give exact answers.
    🔗
    Answer.
    For coffee, \(5.0 = -\log[H^+]\text{,}\) so \(\log[H^+] = -5\) and \([H^+] = 10^{-5}\) moles per liter. For lemon juice, \([H^+] = 10^{-2.3} \approx 0.0050\) moles per liter.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    How many times more acidic is lemon juice than black coffee? (Compare the hydrogen ion concentrations.)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\frac{10^{-2.3}}{10^{-5}} = 10^{-2.3 - (-5)} = 10^{2.7} \approx 501\text{.}\) Lemon juice is about \(500\) times more acidic, even though the pH values differ by only \(2.7\text{.}\) Each drop of \(1\) in pH means \(10\) times more acidic.
