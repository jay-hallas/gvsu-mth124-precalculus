    Worksheet Midterm Review: Fall 2026
    

    This review covers evaluating functions, composition and inverses, average rate of change, linear functions, and circular functions. Show your work on each problem.
    🔗

    1. Problem 1: Evaluating a Function.

    Let \(f(x) = 2x^2 - 3x + 5\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Find \(f(-2)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(f(-2) = 2(-2)^2 - 3(-2) + 5 = 8 + 6 + 5 = 19\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find \(f(2x - 1)\) and simplify.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Replace every \(x\) with \(2x - 1\text{,}\) then expand.
    \begin{align*} f(2x - 1) \amp= 2(2x - 1)^2 - 3(2x - 1) + 5\\ \amp= 2(4x^2 - 4x + 1) - 6x + 3 + 5\\ \amp= 8x^2 - 8x + 2 - 6x + 8\\ \amp= 8x^2 - 14x + 10 \end{align*}
    🔗
    As a check, \(x = 0\) gives \(10\text{,}\) and \(f(-1) = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Problem 2: Functions Given by a Table.

    The table below gives some values of the functions \(f\) and \(g\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \(x\) \(-2\) \(-1\) \(0\) \(1\) \(2\) \(3\)
    \(f(x)\) \(3\) \(1\) \(-1\) \(2\) \(0\) \(4\)
    \(g(x)\) \(0\) \(2\) \(3\) \(2\) \(-1\) \(1\)

    (a)

    Find \(f(1)\) and \(g(0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(f(1) = 2\) and \(g(0) = 3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find \((f \circ g)(0)\text{,}\) \((g \circ f)(-1)\text{,}\) and \(f(f(2))\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Work from the inside out.
    🔗
    \((f \circ g)(0) = f(g(0)) = f(3) = 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \((g \circ f)(-1) = g(f(-1)) = g(1) = 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \(f(f(2)) = f(0) = -1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Which of \(f\) or \(g\) has an inverse? Explain how you know. For the function that has an inverse, find its inverse evaluated at \(-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(f\) has an inverse because every input gives a different output. \(g\) does not, since \(g(-1) = 2\) and \(g(1) = 2\text{;}\) two different inputs give the same output.
    🔗
    Since \(f(0) = -1\text{,}\) we have \(f^{-1}(-1) = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. Problem 3: Functions and Inverses from Graphs.

    Use the four graphs below to answer the questions.
    🔗
    
    An upward opening parabola with vertex at (0, -2), crossing the x-axis near -1.4 and 1.4.
    🔗
    
    A circle centered at the origin with radius 2.5.
    🔗
    
    A straight line with negative slope, crossing the y-axis at 1 and the x-axis at 2.
    🔗
    
    A parabola opening to the right with vertex at (-2, 0), crossing the y-axis near -1.4 and 1.4.
    🔗

    (a)

    Which graphs are not functions? Explain how you know.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Graphs (b) and (d) are not functions. Each fails the vertical line test; for example, the line \(x = 0\) crosses each of them twice.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Which graphs are functions that have an inverse? Explain how you know.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Graph (c) is a function with an inverse. It passes the vertical line test, and every horizontal line crosses it exactly once, so it passes the horizontal line test.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Which graphs are functions that do not have an inverse? Explain how you know.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Graph (a) is a function, but it fails the horizontal line test. For example, the line \(y = 2\) crosses it at \(x = -2\) and \(x = 2\text{,}\) so it has no inverse.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Problem 4: Average Rate of Change of a Function.

    Let \(f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 1\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the average rate of change of \(f\) on the interval \([1, 4]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(f(1) = 1 - 4 + 1 = -2\) and \(f(4) = 16 - 16 + 1 = 1\text{,}\) so
    \begin{align*} \frac{f(4) - f(1)}{4 - 1} \amp= \frac{1 - (-2)}{3} = 1 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the average rate of change of \(f\) on the interval \([-1, 2]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(f(-1) = 1 + 4 + 1 = 6\) and \(f(2) = 4 - 8 + 1 = -3\text{,}\) so
    \begin{align*} \frac{f(2) - f(-1)}{2 - (-1)} \amp= \frac{-3 - 6}{3} = -3 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. Problem 5: Average Rate of Change in Context.

    A cup of hot tea is left to cool on a counter. The table gives the temperature \(T\) of the tea, in degrees Fahrenheit, \(t\) minutes after it was poured.
    🔗
    \(t\) (minutes) \(0\) \(5\) \(10\) \(15\) \(20\)
    \(T(t)\) (\(^\circ\text{F}\)) \(180\) \(152\) \(132\) \(118\) \(108\)

    (a)

    Find the average rate of change of \(T\) from \(t = 0\) to \(t = 10\text{.}\) Include units. Then write a sentence explaining what your answer means in this context.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\frac{132 - 180}{10 - 0} = \frac{-48}{10} = -4.8\) degrees Fahrenheit per minute.
    🔗
    Over the first 10 minutes, the tea cooled by an average of 4.8 degrees per minute.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the average rate of change of \(T\) from \(t = 10\) to \(t = 20\text{.}\) Include units. Then write a sentence explaining what your answer means in this context.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\frac{108 - 132}{20 - 10} = \frac{-24}{10} = -2.4\) degrees Fahrenheit per minute.
    🔗
    Between 10 and 20 minutes after it was poured, the tea cooled by an average of 2.4 degrees per minute.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Compare your two answers. What do they tell you about how the tea is cooling?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The tea is still cooling, but more slowly as time goes on; it lost about half as many degrees per minute in the second 10 minutes as in the first.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Problem 6: A Line Through Two Points.

    A linear function \(f\) passes through the points \((-2, 7)\) and \((4, -2)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Write an equation for \(f\) in point-slope form.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The slope is \(m = \frac{-2 - 7}{4 - (-2)} = \frac{-9}{6} = -\frac{3}{2}\text{.}\) Using the point \((-2, 7)\text{,}\)
    \begin{align*} y - 7 \amp= -\frac{3}{2}(x + 2) \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Write an equation for \(f\) in slope-intercept form.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Distribute and solve for \(y\text{.}\)
    \begin{align*} y - 7 \amp= -\frac{3}{2}x - 3\\ y \amp= -\frac{3}{2}x + 4 \end{align*}
    🔗
    As a check, \(f(4) = -6 + 4 = -2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find a formula for \(f^{-1}(x)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Swap \(x\) and \(y\text{,}\) then solve for \(y\text{.}\)
    \begin{align*} x \amp= -\frac{3}{2}y + 4\\ x - 4 \amp= -\frac{3}{2}y\\ y \amp= -\frac{2}{3}(x - 4) = -\frac{2}{3}x + \frac{8}{3} \end{align*}
    🔗
    So \(f^{-1}(x) = -\frac{2}{3}x + \frac{8}{3}\text{.}\) As a check, \(f(4) = -2\) and \(f^{-1}(-2) = \frac{4}{3} + \frac{8}{3} = 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    7. Problem 7: A Draining Tank.

    A tank holds \(450\) gallons of water. A valve is opened, and water drains out at a constant rate of \(15\) gallons per minute. Let \(W(t)\) be the amount of water in the tank, in gallons, \(t\) minutes after the valve is opened.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find a formula for \(W(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The initial value is \(450\) and the rate of change is \(-15\) gallons per minute, so \(W(t) = 450 - 15t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find \(W(12)\) and explain what it means in this context.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(W(12) = 450 - 15(12) = 270\text{.}\) After 12 minutes, there are 270 gallons of water left in the tank.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    How long does it take for the tank to empty?
    🔗
    Answer.
    Set \(W(t) = 0\text{:}\) \(450 - 15t = 0\text{,}\) so \(t = 30\text{.}\) The tank is empty after 30 minutes.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    8. Problem 8: Cab Fare.

    A cab company charges a fixed fee plus a set amount per mile. A \(4\)-mile ride costs \(\$13.50\text{,}\) and a \(10\)-mile ride costs \(\$27.00\text{.}\) Let \(C(m)\) be the cost, in dollars, of an \(m\)-mile ride.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find a formula for \(C(m)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The slope is \(\frac{27.00 - 13.50}{10 - 4} = \frac{13.50}{6} = 2.25\text{.}\) Then \(C(m) = 2.25m + b\text{,}\) and using \(C(4) = 13.50\text{,}\)
    \begin{align*} 13.50 \amp= 2.25(4) + b\\ b \amp= 4.50 \end{align*}
    🔗
    So \(C(m) = 2.25m + 4.50\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Write a sentence explaining what the slope means in this context. Include units.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Each additional mile adds \(\$2.25\) to the cost of the ride.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    What does the vertical intercept of \(C\) mean in this context? How much does a \(15\)-mile ride cost?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The vertical intercept, \(\$4.50\text{,}\) is the fixed fee charged for every ride before any miles are driven. A 15-mile ride costs \(C(15) = 2.25(15) + 4.50 = \$38.25\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    9. Problem 9: Reading a Circular Function Graph.

    The graph of a circular function \(y = f(x)\) is shown below.
    🔗
    
    A smooth wave on a grid. It starts at the point (0, 1), rises to a maximum at (3, 5), falls to a minimum at (6, 1), rises to a maximum at (9, 5), and falls back to (12, 1).
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the maximum value and the minimum value of \(f\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The maximum value is \(5\) (at \(x = 3\) and \(x = 9\)). The minimum value is \(1\) (at \(x = 0\text{,}\) \(6\text{,}\) and \(12\)).
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the equation of the midline and the amplitude.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The midline is halfway between the max and min: \(y = \frac{5 + 1}{2} = 3\text{.}\) The amplitude is the distance from the midline to the max: \(\frac{5 - 1}{2} = 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find the period.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The graph goes from one minimum at \(x = 0\) to the next at \(x = 6\text{,}\) so the period is \(6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    10. Problem 10: A Ferris Wheel.

    This problem does not include a picture. Draw one before you start.
    🔗
    A Ferris wheel has a diameter of \(50\) feet, and its center is \(30\) feet above the ground. The wheel makes one full turn every \(8\) minutes. You board at the lowest point of the wheel at time \(t = 0\text{.}\) Let \(h(t)\) be your height above the ground, in feet, after \(t\) minutes.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the amplitude, midline, and period of \(h\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The amplitude is the radius, \(25\) feet. The midline is the height of the center, \(y = 30\text{.}\) The period is \(8\) minutes.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Should the model start with \(\sin\text{,}\) \(\cos\text{,}\) \(-\sin\text{,}\) or \(-\cos\text{?}\) Explain your choice.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(-\cos\text{.}\) At \(t = 0\) you are at the lowest point, and \(-\cos\) is the one that starts at its minimum.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Write a formula for \(h(t)\text{.}\) Then find your height after \(2\) minutes.
    🔗
    Answer.
    With period \(8\text{,}\) \(B = \frac{2\pi}{8} = \frac{\pi}{4}\text{,}\) so
    \begin{align*} h(t) \amp= -25\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}t\right) + 30 \end{align*}
    🔗
    \(h(2) = -25\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) + 30 = 0 + 30 = 30\) feet. This makes sense; after a quarter turn you are level with the center.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Suppose instead that at \(t = 0\) you are level with the center of the wheel and moving upward. Which of \(\sin\text{,}\) \(\cos\text{,}\) \(-\sin\text{,}\) or \(-\cos\) would you use now? Write the new formula.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\sin\text{,}\) since it starts on the midline and increases. The new formula is \(h(t) = 25\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}t\right) + 30\text{.}\)
